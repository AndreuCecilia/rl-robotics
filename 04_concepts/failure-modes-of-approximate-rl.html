Failure modes of approximate RL

A course-specific list of six ways approximate RL breaks down, used from Week 7 onward as the organising question at the start of every theory session.

Where it sits

  • Unifying diagram cell: cross-cutting, since a method’s cell records what it represents and this list records what goes wrong with it
  • Course first appearance: Week 7, at the end of the session that formalizes mode 1
  • Recurs: every theory session from Week 8, opening with one question, which failure mode does today’s method address?

Mechanism of mode 2, as Week 9 states it

The multiplier attached to one decision in a policy-gradient update is the return over the whole remaining episode, so it carries the randomness of transitions the decision did not cause and of later actions it did not take. Two rollouts from the same state under the same policy can differ by more than a good action differs from a bad one, and the estimator cannot tell the two apart. Measured at a frozen policy on the 961-state swing-up, one episode of the reward-to-go estimator has Var(g^​)/∥Eg^​∥2=2.0×103. A state-dependent baseline removes the state’s offset and buys 11.3×, and the residual spread of Gt​ around Vπ(xt​) survives it, which is what Week 10 attacks by accepting bias.

Why Week 10 highlights two modes at once

Week 10 is the first session to mark two of the six on the figure, and the mark is a claim about mechanism rather than a summary of coverage. One construction reaches both. Replacing the sampled tail of the episode by a Critic’s prediction removes the dominant source of spread from the policy-gradient estimator, which is mode 2, and it divides the variance of one episode’s estimate by 1.0×103 on the swing-up. The actor is still moved by a gradient step, so no maximisation propagates through a function approximator, which removes one amplifier of mode 1 and replaces T by the milder Tπ. Neither is a repair. Mode 2 survives at a reduced level and is traded against a bias equal to the critic’s error, and mode 1 keeps two of its three ingredients, since Bootstrapping returns with the critic and Baird’s counterexample needs no maximisation to diverge. Mode 4 is untouched by the construction, and SAC is the one method in that week’s design space that addresses it, by putting an entropy term into the objective.

The caveat that travels with the list

This is not a complete taxonomy. It privileges the failure modes that organise this course, and several real ones sit outside it: non-stationarity in deployment, partial observability (POMDP, touched and not centered), multi-agent dynamics (out of scope), and reproducibility together with hyperparameter sensitivity (Week 15). A method that addresses none of the six can still be the right method for a particular robot.

What “addresses” means

The verb is weak on purpose. Week 7 makes mode 1 survivable and removes no ingredient of the Deadly triad, recovers no theorem, and each of its mechanisms is dated to a specific instability somebody observed. Tagging a method with a failure mode means that much for the rest of the course, and no more.
