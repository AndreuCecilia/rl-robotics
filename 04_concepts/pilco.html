PILCO

Spend the model as a gradient: fit a Gaussian process to the dynamics, propagate a Gaussian state distribution through it by moment matching, and differentiate the expected cost with respect to the policy parameters analytically.

Where it sits

  • Unifying diagram cell: a policy × a learned model, the middle column’s policy row
  • Bellman object approximated: none. The objective is differentiated directly, as in Policy gradient (REINFORCE), with the model supplying the expectation the plant would otherwise have to sample
  • Failure mode addressed: sample efficiency, and failure mode 5 in part, since averaging over the posterior charges the policy improvement for regions the data have not pinned down
  • Course first appearance: Week 11, as a sketch and a landmark

Core idea

Once a model exists, the policy can be improved without touching the plant. PILCO takes that to its limit by making the whole rollout differentiable and by carrying the model’s own uncertainty through it, so a policy that would only work if the model happened to be right is penalised during the improvement step rather than after deployment.

Formal definition

Fit a Gaussian process posterior over the dynamics from the data collected so far. Starting from a Gaussian p(x0​), propagate the state distribution H steps by moment matching, approximating each one-step predictive distribution by the Gaussian with the same mean and covariance, which keeps the propagated law Gaussian by construction. Then evaluate and differentiate
J(θ)=∑i=0H−1​Exi​∼pθ​(xi​)​[ℓ(xi​)],∇θ​J computed analytically through the chain of moments,
and take a gradient step on θ. The expectation is taken over the model posterior as well as over the state, which is where the caution comes from.

Anchor numbers

Cart-pole swing-up from scratch in about 20 seconds of interaction, at a time when model-free methods needed hours. The mechanism behind that figure is the posterior average, which makes the policy improvement cautious about regions the data have not pinned down. Ensembles of networks are the current cheap version of the same idea.

Connection to classical control

This is a stochastic optimal control problem solved on the identified plant, with the twist that the uncertainty being propagated is uncertainty about the model rather than about a disturbance. Week 2 settled the disturbance case, where zero-mean noise leaves the LQR gain unchanged, and Certainty equivalence is the name for ignoring the model uncertainty as well. PILCO refuses that second step, which puts it on the same side of the line as robust and stochastic MPC, and it pays the same currency, conservatism.

Key limitation

Moment matching assumes the propagated distribution stays Gaussian, which contact breaks. The Gaussian process cost grows with the size of the data set, so the method is at its best exactly where data are scarce. Both limits are structural rather than implementation defects, which is why the course keeps the idea and drops the algorithm. What survived is the claim that a model should report its own uncertainty and that a planner should be charged for using regions where that uncertainty is large.
