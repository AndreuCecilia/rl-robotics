Actor-critic

A parameterised policy improved by gradient ascent on J, with the multiplier on each score supplied by a bootstrapped value estimate of the policy being improved: the two branches of the course composed, one holding what the other lacks.

Where it sits

  • Unifying diagram cell: both × no dynamics model, the bottom-right cell
  • Bellman object approximated: Tπ for the Critic, on sampled transitions, and none for the actor, which is improved by differentiating J directly
  • Failure modes addressed: 1 and 2, by one construction. The critic replaces a sampled tail with a prediction, which takes the variance out of the estimator, and the actor is moved by a gradient step, so no max propagates through an approximator.
  • Course first appearance: Week 10, as the conclusion of the value and policy arc

Core idea

The value branch updates from one transition and needs a maximisation over actions it cannot afford in continuous control. The policy branch needs no maximisation and cannot form an update until an episode has finished. Each branch holds exactly what the other is short of, so composing them is forced by the two failure modes rather than chosen as a third family of algorithm. What results is Generalised policy iteration at the granularity of a single transition, with both steps approximated.

Formal definition

The actor ascends the advantage form of the Policy gradient theorem,
∇θ​J=Ex∼dγπ​,u∼πθ​​[∇θ​logπθ​(u∣x)Aπ(x,u)],
legitimate because subtracting Vπ(x) from Qπ(x,u) is a Baseline (variance reduction) and changes no expectation. The critic supplies an estimate A^t​ of that multiplier, and every stochastic method in this family is a different choice of A^t​ from the GAE family
A^t​=∑l≥0​(γλ)lδt+l​,δt​=rt+1​+γVw​(xt+1​)−Vw​(xt​),
with λ=0 the one-step residual and λ=1 Week 9’s centred return. The two updates share the scalar δt​, computed once per transition. Read as a prediction error it corrects the critic; read as an advantage estimate it raises or lowers the probability of the action just taken.

Placed on the four-row table the course has kept since Week 3, actor-critic is the row where evaluation is one TD backup per transition and improvement is one gradient step on θ, below policy iteration (exact solve, greedy step), Riccati (Lyapunov equation, gain update) and SARSA (one TD backup, ε-greedy on the table).

Anchor numbers

On the 961-cell swing-up with the policy frozen and a critic pre-fitted on 2×104 transitions, turning λ from 1 to 0 divides the variance of one episode’s gradient estimate by 1.0×103 and raises the squared bias from 9×10−5 to 0.79, a net factor of 949 in mean squared error on this problem. Over a full run with the critic learned online from zero, three arms sharing step size, seeds, budget and rollout code, both critic arms reach the Week 9 baseline’s final policy value on fewer transitions, 1.9× fewer at λ=0.95 and 1.4× at λ=0, and the inter-seed interquartile range at the end of training falls from 0.39 to 0.07 and 0.00. The λ=0 arm also dips to −213 at 9×105 transitions and trails the baseline until 4.3×106 of the 6.2×106 available, because early in a run its critic is far worse than any of the three the frozen measurement scored. Choose λ for the critic that exists at that moment.

Connection to classical control

The scheme is the self-tuning regulator with the plant model replaced by a value function. An estimator is fitted to data the closed loop is producing, a design step recomputes the controller from the current estimate, and that controller changes what the estimator sees next. The classical difficulty transfers with the diagram, and the answer the theory gives is the same separation of rates that adaptive control uses. Convergence results are Two-timescale stochastic approximation results, in which the critic is given the faster step size so that from the actor’s slower vantage it looks converged.

Key limitation

The exact improvement guarantee of the Policy improvement theorem is gone, because the critic is approximate and the step is small rather than greedy. What remains is convergence to a stationary point of a non-concave J under stochastic-approximation conditions, which is weaker than the fixed-point theorems of Weeks 3 and 5 and weaker again than Week 9’s, since the critic’s error enters the direction the actor follows. The construction also puts Bootstrapping back beside Function approximation in RL, so two legs of the Deadly triad are present in every member of the family and the third is held off only while the data for an update come from the policy being updated.
